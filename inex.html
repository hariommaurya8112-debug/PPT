What Is Graphic Presentation?

Graphic presentation means presenting information with charts, graphs, diagrams, symbols, pictures, and infographics instead of showing only rows of numbers.

👁️

See quickly

Understand key information at a glance.

⚖️

Compare values

Find highest, lowest, and differences.

📈

Find patterns

Notice trends and changes over time.

💡

Explain ideas

Share results in a clear and simple way.

Visuals help simplify large amounts of information.

Speaking summary

Graphic presentation is a visual way of explaining data. Charts help us compare values and identify trends quickly. They are useful when the data is too large or confusing to explain only in words.

03 / WHY VISUALS MATTERNUMBERS TO UNDERSTANDING

Objectives & Importance

The main objective is to simplify data and communicate useful information accurately.

  • Summarise large datasets.
  • Compare values and categories.
  • Identify trends, patterns, and unusual results.
  • Support planning and decision-making.
  • Explain technical results to different audiences.
  • Save time during analysis and reporting.

Data → analysis → insight → better decision.

Speaking summary

Graphic presentation reduces complexity, helps us compare values, reveals patterns, and supports decisions. In B.Tech and CSE projects, it helps explain experiment results, application performance, test data, and system behaviour.

Types of Graphs & Charts

▥ Bar Graph

Compares categories such as marks or sales.

⌁ Line Graph

Shows change over time.

◔ Pie Chart

Displays percentages of one total.

▤ Histogram

Groups numbers into ranges.

⁙ Scatter Plot

Shows possible relationships between values.

▰ Area Chart

Highlights volume or change over time.

Speaking summary

Different graphs have different uses. Bar graphs compare categories, line graphs show time trends, pie charts show percentages, histograms show frequency ranges, scatter plots show relationships, and area charts show changes in amount over time.

09 / MORE CHART TYPESDISTRIBUTION + RELATIONSHIP

Histogram & Scatter Plot

Histogram

A histogram groups numerical values into ranges. The bars usually touch because the ranges are continuous.

  • Use it to study distributions.
  • Example: marks grouped into intervals.
  • It helps identify common ranges and unusual values.

Scatter Plot

A scatter plot uses dots to compare two numerical variables.

  • Example: study hours and exam marks.
  • An upward pattern may suggest a positive relationship.
  • Correlation does not automatically prove cause and effect.

A. Marks Distribution

B. Study Hours vs Marks

Speaking summary

A histogram shows how values fall into ranges, and adjacent bars usually touch. A scatter plot shows the relationship between two numerical values. The example suggests that more study hours and higher marks tend to appear together, but it does not prove that study time alone causes a particular result.

13 / COMPUTER SCIENCE EXAMPLENETWORK MONITORING

Network dashboards help engineers monitor how much data moves through a system.

  • Bandwidth: the amount of data transferred in a time period.
  • Latency: the delay before data reaches its destination.
  • Packet loss: data packets that fail to arrive.
  • Error rate: how often requests or operations fail.

Graphs can help identify traffic spikes and possible performance problems.

14 / RESPONSIBLE DESIGNCLARITY OVER DECORATION

Advantages, Limits & Good Graph Design

✅ Advantages

  • Quick and easy to understand.
  • Clear comparison of values.
  • Shows trends and patterns.
  • Makes reports more engaging.
  • Supports informed decisions.

⚠️ Limitations

  • Wrong values or scales can mislead.
  • Too many graphics may confuse viewers.
  • Some tools cost money.

Rules for a Good Graph

  • Give the graph a clear title.
  • Label axes and units.
  • Choose a suitable chart type.
  • Use fair scales and readable colours.
  • Show the source of the data.
  • Check values, labels, and spelling.

The goal is to make the data clearer, not to mislead.

Speaking summary

Graphic presentation makes data faster to understand and easier to compare. However, an incorrect value, unfair scale, or cluttered chart may mislead the audience. A good graph needs a clear title, labelled axes, suitable colours, accurate values, and a reliable data source.
